# A-study-on-prescribing-patterns-of-anti-epileptic-drugs-in-an-out-patient-clinic
Project Report: Analysis of Anti-Epileptic Drug (AED) Prescribing Patterns and Adherence

Project Overview

This project involves analyzing the prescribing patterns of anti-epileptic drugs (AEDs) in a cohort of 100 epilepsy patients through prospective data collection. The study evaluates medication adherence using MMAS-8 (Morisky Medication Adherence Scale) and assesses adverse drug reaction (ADR) causality with Naranjo’s scale. Key findings include levetiracetam as the most prescribed first-line therapy (40%), a high adherence rate of 45%, and a 22% incidence of ADRs.

1. Prescribing Patterns of Anti-Epileptic Drugs (AEDs)

Objective

To identify the most commonly prescribed AEDs and assess treatment trends in epilepsy management.

Methodology

Data Collection: Prospective study involving 100 epilepsy patients.

Analysis Criteria: Classification based on first-line and adjunct AED therapy.

Evaluation Parameters: Frequency of prescribed AEDs, monotherapy vs. polytherapy usage.

Key Findings

Levetiracetam was the most commonly prescribed first-line therapy (40%).

Other prescribed AEDs:

Valproate (25%)

Carbamazepine (15%)

Lamotrigine (10%)

Others (10%)

Monotherapy vs. Polytherapy:

60% of patients were on monotherapy.

40% received polytherapy.

Conclusion

Levetiracetam’s high prescription rate suggests its preference as a first-line AED due to efficacy and safety profile. The study underscores the importance of personalized treatment approaches in epilepsy management.

2. Evaluation of Medication Adherence

Objective

To assess medication adherence among epilepsy patients using the MMAS-8 scale.

Methodology

Adherence Assessment Tool: MMAS-8 questionnaire.

Classification:

High adherence: Score ≥ 8

Medium adherence: Score 6-7

Low adherence: Score < 6

Key Findings

45% of patients exhibited high adherence.

30% of patients had moderate adherence.

25% of patients showed low adherence.

Conclusion

The study highlights the need for targeted interventions to improve adherence in epilepsy patients, such as patient education and medication counseling.

3. Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) Causality Analysis

Objective

To evaluate the incidence and causality of ADRs in epilepsy patients using Naranjo’s scale.

Methodology

ADR Assessment Tool: Naranjo’s ADR probability scale.

Classification:

Definite ADR: Score ≥ 9

Probable ADR: Score 5-8

Possible ADR: Score 1-4

Key Findings

22% of patients experienced ADRs.

Common ADRs included:

Drowsiness (8%)

Mood changes (6%)

Gastrointestinal disturbances (5%)

Rash (3%)

ADR causality assessment:

50% were categorized as probable ADRs.

30% were classified as possible ADRs.

20% were definite ADRs.

Conclusion

Monitoring and reporting ADRs are crucial in optimizing AED therapy. Strategies such as dose adjustments and alternative drug selections may help mitigate adverse effects.

Conclusion & Future Directions

This study provides valuable insights into AED prescribing patterns, medication adherence, and ADR incidences in epilepsy patients. Key recommendations include:

Enhancing adherence strategies through patient education.

Regular monitoring of ADRs for better treatment outcomes.

Further research with larger sample sizes to validate findings.
